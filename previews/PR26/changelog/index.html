Changelog

Unreleased

Correctness

  • Residuals are now measured against the spectral scale of the pencil, ‖Ax - λBx‖ / (‖Bx‖ max(|λ|, σ)), instead of a fixed floor of 1. The old floor made the convergence test absolute for small-magnitude problems, so a change of units could turn a converged solve into one that accepted inaccurate or spurious eigenpairs with info = 0. σ is measured on random probes before the first contour sweep, which adds one MULT_A/MULT_B request at the start of every RCI loop; callers that answer those requests generically need no change.
  • Spurious Ritz pairs — mixtures of out-of-region eigenvectors whose Rayleigh quotient falls inside the region — are recognized by their filter response and no longer counted. They used to block convergence for the whole loop budget (info = 5) or be returned as eigenvalues, including by eigvals_feast and eigen_feast.
  • For non-Hermitian and complex-symmetric problems, a spurious pair built by the oblique Rayleigh-Ritz from an in-region eigenvector plus an unresolved conjugate pair is also recognized: the filter restricted to the subspace shows no more in-region directions than converged pairs.
  • Polynomial eigenproblems no longer depend on units. The companion-based solvers (feast_polynomial, feast_gepev! and relatives, the sparse *csrpev! family) balance the coefficients before linearizing, and the moment-method kernel behind feast_srcipev!/feast_grcipev! measures residuals as backward errors ‖P(λ)x‖ / (Σ|λ|ᵏ‖Aₖ‖ ‖x‖). That kernel measures the coefficient sizes with extra MULT_A requests before its first factorization. validate_companion_matrices judges residuals relative to the size of their terms.
  • A search region with no eigenvalues now returns info = 0 with M = 0 instead of info = 5. A caller-supplied starting subspace is first checked with independent probes.
  • eigvals_feast and eigen_feast with check=false log a warning when the solve did not converge.
  • The generalized interval check bounds the spectrum of the pencil (A, B), not of A, so it no longer warns about intervals that contain eigenvalues.
  • feast_matvec reaches the default tolerance (its shifted solves are warm-started, see below); its GMRES options are now keywords.
  • The sparse complex-symmetric solver keeps a caller's initial_subspace instead of overwriting it.
  • An empty Ritz set is taken as an empty search region only once the subspace has converged outside it: the nearest Ritz pair has converged and stopped moving, the filter is negligible wherever the Ritz values are, or the filtered block itself is negligible. For a non-Hermitian pencil the settled subspace must also hold a converged pair that the filter amplifies less than anything inside the region, or have been kept less than that as a whole, because an eigenvalue just outside the contour, near a node, can be amplified more than the region itself. This applies to every driver. It fixes info = 0, M = 0 for intervals beside more eigenvalues than M0 and for strongly non-normal pencils with M0 below the eigenvalue count (none in 1,200 such fuzz cases, against about 90 before).
  • Defective eigenvalues keep their algebraic multiplicity in the general and complex-symmetric solvers. The Ritz vectors of a Jordan block of size k converge to within about eps^(1/k) of each other, and the rank compression of the next sweep kept only one of them: the general solvers reported fewer eigenvalues than the region holds as a success (every block of size 3 or 4 tested), and the complex-symmetric ones, whose Rayleigh quotient xᵀAx / xᵀx is 0/0 at such an eigenvector, stopped converging. A nearly dependent block of in-region Ritz vectors is now swept through an orthonormal basis of its span. As with LAPACK, the k copies agree only to about eps^(1/k) and their eigenvectors are nearly parallel.
  • The spurious-pair screen compares a pair's filter response with the filter's value at its own Ritz value, so rescaled weights, flat ellipses and few-node contours keep their eigenpairs. Near a node of a full contour, where that value peaks, a pair is held to the filter's floor inside the region instead. Pairs within sqrt(tol) of converging, and pairs whose residual bound places an eigenvalue inside the region, are never screened.
  • Zolotarev quadrature (fpm[16] = 2) applies its constant term in every Hermitian driver, so the filter is the tabulated rational function. Without it the filter's worst response outside the interval was about twice as large relative to its response inside.
  • The polynomial moment kernel (feast_srcipev!, feast_grcipev! and their x and iterative twins) now refines: each loop corrects every Ritz pair through its residual instead of reproducing the first sweep's quadrature error. An empty contour is recognized from the moments of every order up to d - 1; when S0 alone cancels (roots inside together with their negatives), the kernel reports info = 5 after the first sweep.
  • Iterative shifted solves (difeast_*, solver=:gmres, and matrix-free solvers made by create_iterative_solver) are warm-started from the trial vectors with a fixed default inner tolerance of 1e-3. They solve for a correction that shrinks with the eigen-residual, so they no longer stall short of the default outer tolerance. The matrix-free drivers take a warm_start keyword.
  • The threaded and real MPI drivers run as many refinement loops as the serial drivers; they ran one fewer.
  • Exhausting the loop budget before verifying a user seed reports info = 5. The result includes unresolved Ritz candidates and recovers saved converged pairs if the check found fewer directions.
  • feast_circle and feast_ellipse select by the shape they describe. FeastParameters() means all defaults; it used to set the tolerance to 1. Threaded backends honour a registered custom contour. Closed contours are no longer doubled by Hermitian completion.
  • feast_customcontour uses spectrally accurate trapezoidal weights. The central differences used before scaled the filter by 0.90 on an 8-node circle, and two nodes gave zero weights. Translating the nodes leaves the weights unchanged to working precision. It needs at least three nodes and records the count in both fpm[2] and fpm[8]. Registered contours use their supplied node counts.
  • feast_hbev! returns info = 2 when M0 is too small, like the other drivers. An explicit Float32 tolerance such as fpm[3] = 6 is honoured. feastinit_driver(N) always produces a node count that feastdefault! accepts, and feast_scsrgv_iterative! resolves defaults before reading the tolerance.

Inputs

  • Sparse matrices with Int32 indices, mixed dense/sparse pencils such as feast(sparse(A), Diagonal(b), interval), Rational and Complex{Int} matrices, a real centre in feast_general, integer storage in feast_banded, contour objects in eigvals_feast and eigen_feast, and custom contours with any node count in the x drivers now work. feast_polynomial with its default M0 works on problems smaller than 10.
  • SymTridiagonal, Tridiagonal, Bidiagonal, Diagonal, and Symmetric, Hermitian, adjoint and transpose wrappers of sparse matrices keep sparse storage instead of being densified. The interval check of a wrapped sparse matrix costs O(nnz) instead of O(N²).
  • Unsupported element types such as BigFloat raise an ArgumentError that says what to convert to; they used to end in info = 8. NaN or infinite intervals are argument errors.
  • Interval solvers accept one or two half-contour points.

Parallel execution

  • backend=:distributed, and :auto once workers exist, runs dense real symmetric problems on the workers instead of serially. feast_parallel_comparison skips backends that do not support the problem instead of throwing. The distributed RCI path keeps each worker's factorizations for the whole solve.
  • Overlapping threaded solves no longer leave BLAS at one thread. feast_with_backend(...; strict_backend=true) throws when MPI is unavailable.

Performance

  • create_iterative_solver no longer makes a dynamic call per right-hand-side element, which halves the allocations of a small matrix-free GMRES solve. The matrix-free drivers form B*Q once per contour sweep instead of at every contour point.
  • feast_parallel starts from the default subspace like the serial solvers. It used to treat that subspace as one supplied by the caller and check it with independent probes before accepting the answer, which took one or two extra refinement loops per solve: twice the serial loop count on easy problems. A subspace a caller passes to pfeast_srci! in work is still checked; set state.user_subspace = false to start from the default one.
  • The threaded, distributed and MPI real symmetric solvers (backend=:threads, :distributed and :mpi, pfeast_sygv!, pfeast_scsrgv!, mpi_feast and feast_hybrid) keep the trial subspace and the reduced eigenproblem real, as the serial solvers do; only the shifted solves are complex. A product of a real dense matrix with a complex block does not use BLAS: at N = 2000 the three such products of a refinement loop took longer than all of its contour solves. The MPI solvers reduce half as much data per sweep, and the threaded and distributed solvers start from the serial solvers' subspace.

Behaviour changes

  • General banded drivers (feast_gbev! and relatives) need 2ka+1 rows and reject upper-only storage.
  • Zolotarev quadrature is restricted to its tabulated counts (1-8, 10, 12, 16); other counts throw instead of using a broken fallback.
  • An empty region beside more eigenvalues than M0 columns can end as info = 2 or 5 with M = 0 instead of info = 0. This happens hard against a cluster on the real axis, or beside an eigenvalue just outside a complex contour near a node. The solver cannot tell such a region from one whose eigenvalues it has not reached; increase M0.
  • feast(A, sparse(B), interval; backend=:threads) solves instead of throwing.
  • mixed_precision=true densifies banded structured matrices and still rejects sparse storage.
  • A failing matrix-free solver callback logs a warning instead of silently returning info = 8.
  • Custom callbacks passed to the matrix-free drivers still receive B*X. Solvers made by create_iterative_solver receive warm-started right-hand sides; warm_start=false restores B*X.

Other fixes

  • MPI is detected automatically again when FEASTKIT_ENABLE_MPI=true, MPI is loaded and initialized; the check used to run before the extension loaded, so it never succeeded. Calling an MPI entry point without using MPI now says what to load.
  • Added FeastLinearOperator, an unambiguous alias for LinearOperator when LinearOperators.jl is also loaded.
  • ParallelFeastState accepts the unset contour count of feastinit().fpm.
  • pfeast_rci_benchmark reports the number of integration points its runs use; it printed 0.
  • Fixed the README examples, which are now executed by the test suite together with the example scripts; documented the differences from Fortran FEAST.
  • Removed the unused SharedArrays dependency.
  • feast_memory_estimate no longer overflows for very large problems.
  • feast_contour and feast_gcontour validate fpm[16] and fpm[18]; feast_gcontour rejects Zolotarev, which is defined for intervals only.
  • feast_inside_contourx tests the polygon through the nodes; it accepted points in the notches of a concave contour (anywhere in the nodes' convex hull). It rejects a contour with no nodes.

Earlier unreleased changes

  • Reuse GMRES workspaces across contour nodes and sweeps; reuse matrix-free BiCGSTAB workspaces across right-hand sides and shifts.

  • Default inner tolerances in general, complex-symmetric, banded, matrix-free, and complex MPI solves (now a fixed 1e-3 on warm-started solves, see above); explicit tolerances remain fixed.

  • Add serial/matrix-free initial_subspace warm starts, opt-in subspace_size=:auto with max_subspace_size, and full-contour count estimation.

  • Add opt-in dense serial mixed precision with Float64 residual checks and fallback. fpm[42] now defaults to 0; unsupported drivers reject 1.

  • Added named high-level solver controls, direct full-contour overloads, and readable result aliases and status messages.

  • Added optional check=true to eigvals_feast and eigen_feast.

  • Corrected threaded projection, generalized residual scaling, rectangle membership near corners, and worker-local distributed factorizations.

  • Updated the web documentation and executable examples to match these APIs.
